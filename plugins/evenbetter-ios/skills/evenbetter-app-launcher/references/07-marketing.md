## 1. Marketing philosophy

Your marketing will only take you as far as your idea allows. If the product is the Purple Cow (remarkable), marketing becomes leverage. If it isn't, the marketing itself has to be the Purple Cow.

Key principle: The "gotcha moment" drives everything. At least 50% of your time should be on creating and refining it. Influencers show your app for 10-15 seconds, and in that window the viewer has to see the benefit.

## 2. Organic content

Organic content is a powerful way to validate your idea and evaluate the effectiveness of your gotcha moment. You should be able to generate noticeable views based solely on your app's key functionality.

### What works

**Instagram:**
- Humorous spin on app functionality
- Strong call-to-action in caption (e.g., "Comment 'BETA' for early access")
- Shows the gotcha moment in action

**TikTok:**
- Decent hook + slightly dramatic tone
- Shows passion for the product
- Conveys authenticity

### Pros
- Can build a strong, almost cult-like following for free
- Validates your gotcha moment

### Cons
- Requires skill at creating this content style
- Performance can be inconsistent
- Play to your strengths: if you're better at directing influencers, focus there

## 3. Influencer marketing strategy

This is the primary growth lever for consumer apps. Recurring influencer posts raise your floor revenue every week.

### Why influencer marketing works
- Influencers have engaged audiences in specific niches
- Content feels authentic vs. traditional ads
- Can achieve $2-5 CPM (very efficient)
- Long-term partnerships create consistent growth

## 4. Finding influencers

### Method 1: Organic feed engineering (higher quality)
1. Create a new social media account
2. Behave as if you were your target customer
3. Like content they would like
4. Watch videos all the way through
5. The algorithm will surface relevant influencers

Takes longer, but every influencer found is highly relevant.

### Method 2: VA outreach (higher volume)
1. Create criteria: follower count, average views, engagement, posting frequency
2. Define hashtags your target audience interacts with
3. Hire a virtual assistant (VA) on Upwork or a similar site, and agree a price per 100 DMs up front
4. Have them run outreach from your account

Gives volume but floods inbox with unqualified leads.

### Green flags in an influencer
- 25k+ views per video
- Stable views never dipping under 15k
- Content performs on multiple platforms
- Unique style (not just trend-hopping)
- Comments show engaged community

### Red flags
- Inconsistent views (100k one video, 5k the next)
- Trend hopper: copies popular sounds, no distinct style
- Comments are just emojis (low-quality viewers or fake engagement)
- High views but no comments (viewers not engaged enough to act)

**Remember: Followers don't matter! Only views & engagement do.**

## 5. Outreach and DMs

### Setting up professional outreach account
1. Create a dedicated account
2. Bio clearly communicates: who you are, what you're building
3. Build a real following first; a few thousand followers makes DMs look credible. Do not buy followers: the US FTC's rule on reviews and testimonials bans buying fake followers or views for a commercial purpose, and platforms remove them
4. Consider the platform's paid verification badge (for example Meta Verified), which stands out in a DM inbox. Check the current price on the platform

### Crafting the DM

**Structure:**
1. **First two words:** "Paid promo?" gets immediate attention
2. **One sentence:** What your app does (don't overexplain)
3. **Clear invitation:** "I'd love to sponsor you!"

**Example:**
> "Paid promo for Arc? Hey, I run an app that limits screen time and unlocks usage when goals are completed. I love your content and would love to sponsor you! Let me know if you're interested."

They get thousands of DMs and won't read a long one. They do answer when they can see you'll pay.

## 6. Negotiating deals

### Rule #1: Get on the phone
- DM as little as possible; every DM should steer toward a phone call
- Keep payment numbers out of text, so the first number comes up on the call where you can frame it

**If they say "tell me more":**
> "Absolutely, I'd love to hop on a call and walk you through the integration. Here's my number: (xxx-xxx-xxxx)."

**If they ask for price:**
> "Before we get into numbers, I'd like to show you what we want demonstrated in the video."

### On the phone

**Opening:**
- Friendly tone
- Compliment one specific aspect of their content (it shows you're a fan, not mass-messaging)

**Explain the integration:**
- How the app appears naturally in their content
- Share examples or past influencer videos
- Provide visual assets if needed

**Discuss rates:**
Ask: "What do you feel is a reasonable rate?"

Then determine value by average view count:
- Take their average views per video
- Underestimate conservatively
- Multiply by number of videos

**Example deal:**
- Average 25k views/video, you want 4 posts
- Conservatively project 100k total views
- Offer: $250 for 4 videos ($2.50 CPM)
- Minimum view guarantee of 100k views
- Pay 50% up front / 50% upon completion + guarantee met
- If they don't hit the guarantee: keep posting until they do

### If they want more money
- Move to $350 flat
- Or propose a CPM model: $2-$5 per 1,000 views with a cap

**Example negotiation flow:**
> Influencer: "$350 for 4 videos is too low."
> You: "I need to stay in the $2-3 CPM range. What I could do is pay you $3 per thousand views. If 4 videos generate 200k views total, that's $600. But there'd be a cap of $500 per video."

This switches their frame from "$350 total" to "potentially $2,000 if I perform well."

### After you present your offer, stop talking
You are the buyer. They need the sponsor as much as you need the promotion.

## 7. Creating winning creatives

### Core principle: Content should not feel like an ad
It should feel like the influencer genuinely uses your app. It still has to be labelled as paid; see section 10.

### How to make it feel real
1. **Study their content.** Watch the last 20 or so posts and note what performs.
2. **Use their best format.** Don't force a new style.
3. **Make the app a moment, not the topic.** It appears naturally while they're doing something.
4. **Keep their voice.** Same language, energy and personality.
5. **The influencer is the hero, the app is a tool.** They aren't promoting it, they're using it.

### Format options
- **Day in the Life:** App appears during daily actions
- **Routine Videos:** App becomes part of a habit
- **Educational/Tip Content:** App is a recommended tool
- **Comedy/Skits:** App is a prop or punchline (it's OK to make fun of it in good fun)
- **Before/After:** App is the quiet reason for improvement
- **Motivational:** App supports their growth story
- **Q&A:** App is the answer to a viewer's question

Choose the format that already gets strong engagement from their audience.

## 8. Meme pages and topic pages

Very cost-effective for generating views. Have pages repost your best-performing content.

### Pricing
- Don't pay more than $50 per post
- CPM should be $0.50-$1.00
- Best pages have almost cult-like communities

### Strategy
- Repost entertaining/funny content related to your app
- Can reframe content as "news headlines" for shareability
- Even if viewers recognize it as an ad, high comment volume drives engagement

### Case study
Paid a kickboxer $350 to film himself using the app, then reposted on a meme page. Result: grew from $500/month to $1,800/month in the first month.

## 9. Post-campaign optimization

### After the influencer posts
- Monitor analytics; the deal should pay for itself in 1-3 days
- Comment from the company account, which creates a funnel
- Do not plant comments from friends posing as strangers. The FTC's Endorsement Guides count a business, family or personal relationship as a material connection that has to be disclosed ([16 CFR 255.5](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-255/section-255.5)), and its 2024 rule bans undisclosed testimonials from company insiders

### The funnel
Viewer watches influencer → clicks comments → sees your account → watches your videos → downloads the app → primed to convert

### Long-term partnership pitch
Always mention you're focused on long-term partnerships, not one-off posts. Benefits:
1. Recurring posts raise your floor revenue weekly
2. Influencers love consistent monetization over one-time payments

### Common objections and responses

**"XYZ brand paid me thousands for a post."**
> Response: "Are you still working with them?" (They usually aren't. Big brands do one-offs, and you're offering consistent income.)

**"Sponsorship content hurts engagement."**
> Response: "That's why we want a subtle integration into content that already performs. Our best results come when content looks authentic and people naturally comment about the app."

## 10. Disclosure and platform rules

- Mark every paid post as paid, with the platform's paid-partnership label or a clear "#ad". In the US the FTC's Endorsement Guides require influencers to disclose a material connection to a brand, and the major platforms require their labels on branded content.
- Never buy followers, views or reviews. The FTC's rule on consumer reviews and testimonials (announced 2024-08-14) bans buying fake indicators of social media influence, such as bot-generated followers or views, for a commercial purpose, and bans fake or undisclosed insider reviews ([FTC announcement](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials)).
- App Store ratings follow Apple's rules too: ask only through the system review prompt (guideline 5.6.1), and never pay or reward people for ratings. See `references/08-post-launch.md`.
- Outside the US, check the local advertising rules before running a campaign there.
